Method

What this calculator does

It computes the flexural stress in a concrete pavement slab under the combined action of axle loading and temperature warping, expresses that stress as a ratio of the modulus of rupture, determines the allowable number of load repetitions from the fatigue relationship, and reports the cumulative fatigue damage.

Both cracking mechanisms are checked. Bottom-up cracking (BUC) combines the daytime positive temperature differential with the load stress at the slab edge. Top-down cracking (TDC) combines the night-time reversed differential with an axle placed near the transverse joint. The governing mechanism is the one producing the higher stress ratio.

Why thermal warping must not be omitted

A load-only analysis can understate the stress ratio by 40 per cent or more for typical Indian slab geometry and temperature differentials. Because the fatigue relationship is steeply non-linear in the stress ratio, that understatement translates into an order-of-magnitude error in the allowable repetitions. Warping is not a refinement; it frequently governs.
