# STAGE 0 — CUSTOMER VALIDATION INTERVIEW SCRIPT

**Objective:** confirm or destroy the ₹1.5–6 lakh trial-batch pain hypothesis **before**
a single line of product code. Cost: two weeks and zero rupees.
**Kill criterion:** <5 of 10 confirm both the pain **and** a budget to solve it.

---

## RULES OF ENGAGEMENT (read before every call)

1. **Never pitch.** The moment you describe ConcApp, they start being polite and the data
   dies. If asked, say: *"I'm a civil engineer researching how mixes get developed. I'm not
   selling anything."* That is true at this stage.
2. **Ask about the past, not the future.** *"What did you do last time?"* is evidence.
   *"Would you use this?"* is fiction — everyone says yes and nobody buys.
3. **Chase numbers.** Rupees, days, counts. A story without a number is not validation.
4. **Silence is a tool.** After they answer, wait three seconds. The second sentence is
   usually the honest one.
5. **Write it down within 10 minutes of hanging up**, into `docs/stage0/responses/`.

## SECTION A — WARM-UP (2 min, establishes their context)

1. What does your unit produce, and roughly what volume per month?
2. How many distinct mixes do you run?
3. Who decides the mix — you, a consultant, or the client's specification?

*Listening for:* whether they control their own mix. **If they don't, they cannot buy.**
Note it and continue — that itself is a finding.

---

## SECTION B — THE CORE QUESTION: TRIAL COST 🎯

*This section decides the venture. Get numbers.*

4. **Think of the last time you developed a new mix or had to change one. Walk me through
   what happened.** *(Let them talk. Do not interrupt.)*
5. **How many trial batches did it take before you were satisfied?**
6. **What does one trial batch cost you — materials, cubes, testing, labour, everything?**
   - If they hesitate: *"Roughly? Five thousand? Twenty?"* — anchoring gets a number.
7. **How long did the whole thing take, start to finish?**
8. What made you change the mix in the first place? *(cost / new client spec / material
   unavailable / failure / regulation)*
9. **Has a mix ever failed after you'd approved it? What did that cost?**

*Record verbatim:* trials `___` × cost `₹___` = **`₹___`** over `___` weeks.

> **Hypothesis under test:** 15–40 trials, ₹8–20k each, 3–9 months → ₹1.5–6 lakh.
> **KILL if:** median comes back under ₹5,000/trial **and** under 10 trials.

---

## SECTION C — GEOPOLYMER / LOW-CARBON DEMAND

10. Have you ever been asked — by a client, consultant or authority — for low-carbon,
    green, or fly-ash-based concrete?
11. *(If yes)* What happened? *(If no)* Do you expect that to change in the next 2–3 years?
12. Have you worked with geopolymer or alkali-activated concrete? **What stopped you?**
13. **Where do you buy fly ash / GGBS, and how far away is it? What do you pay including freight?**
14. **And sodium silicate / NaOH — do you have a local supplier? What's the distance and rate?**

> 🎯 **Q13 and Q14 are the most commercially valuable questions in this script.**
> They test finding §0.1 (activators = 47–54% of GPC cost, freight-sensitive) and §0.2
> (fly ash at 96% utilisation). **Every answer is a seed record for the regional cost engine.**
> Ask them of *everyone*, even the ones who've never touched geopolymer.

---

## SECTION D — THE APPROVAL / TRUST QUESTION

15. **If you proposed a mix with no IS code behind it, who has to approve it — and what
    would they need to see?**
16. What documentation do you produce today for a mix design? Who signs it?
17. Have you ever had a mix rejected by a client or consultant? Why?
18. Would you trust a software prediction of 28-day strength? *(Then, crucially:)*
    **What would it have to show you before you'd trust it?**

*Listening for:* the exact contents of the justification dossier (S-06). **They will
describe your product for you.** Write their words down and use them as the spec.

---

## SECTION E — BUDGET & WILLINGNESS TO PAY 💰

19. Do you use any software today for mix design, QC or test records? What do you pay?
20. Who signs off on a software purchase here, and up to what amount without escalation?
21. **If something reliably cut your trials from [their number] to a third, what would that
    be worth to you per mix development?**
    - Then go quiet. **Let them name the number first. Never anchor.**
22. Would you rather pay per mix developed, or a monthly subscription?

> **KILL if:** they cannot name any number, or name it and then say procurement would
> never approve it.

---

## SECTION F — CLOSE

23. What's the single most annoying part of your QC work that nobody has fixed?
    *(Open goal. Sometimes the real business is hiding here — listen hard.)*
24. Who else should I speak to? **Could you introduce me?** *(Always ask. Referrals are
    how you reach 10.)*
25. May I come back to you when I have findings to share?

## AFTER ALL 10 — THE DECISION

Compile into `docs/stage0/FINDINGS.md`:

| Metric | Result | Threshold | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median trials per mix development | | ≥10 | |
| Median cost per trial | | ≥₹5,000 | |
| Median total pain per mix | | ≥₹50,000 | |
| Count confirming pain **and** budget | | **≥5 of 10** | |
| Count who control their own mix | | ≥5 | |
| Count asked for low-carbon concrete | | ≥3 | |

**≥5 qualified → PROCEED to Stage 1.**
**3–4 → the pain is real but narrow. Re-segment and interview 10 more before building.**
**≤2 → STOP. Report to Master. The thesis and research retain full value; three years are saved.**

> Even a "no" outcome delivers: 10 industry relationships, a regional materials-price
> dataset nobody else has, and a thesis chapter on industry barriers to GPC adoption.
> **There is no losing branch in Stage 0.**
